# Historical interpretation without rewriting evidence

A package count and a later conversion definition are separate facts. An unbounded baseline alone cannot prove the definition was known when a historical event occurred. Migration 18 therefore records immutable observations and per-use checks, distinguishing known-before-event, migration-derived and unresolved interpretations. Stable server-stamped legacy flags replace transaction-ID ordering as the classification authority.

Concrete recipe uses require both the originating fulfillment and ordered recipe path. Database validation canonicalizes the allowed representation by rejecting non-one-based or multidimensional nonempty paths, rather than allowing alternate encodings to defeat uniqueness. Direct quantities and recipe uses share an append-only review mechanism with tenant, role, source and prospective-definition checks.

The cutover is captured after locking source and definition tables, so an earlier writer cannot commit a source after the asserted observation boundary while being mislabeled. A fresh pre-18 upgrade test exercises that concurrent writer, preserves source quantities and stored legacy receipt representations/hashes, and confirms migration reentry. Approved-successor backfill retains known-before-event provenance; only the existing baseline receives the qualified migration-derived interpretation.
