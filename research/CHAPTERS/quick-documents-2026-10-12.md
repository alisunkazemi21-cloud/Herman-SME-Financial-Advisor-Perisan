# Request-only documents and the boundary of evidence

C15 connects temporary document reading to the existing Quick Advisor. Persistent business work needs reviewed records and durable receipts; a small standalone question needs bounded context without retaining business history. The intake route returns locally extracted but unverified sources, with no business transaction or stored conversation.

Source references have two identities: the SHA-256 of original bytes and the SHA-256 of each returned text chunk. Page, sheet/row and image locations carry character spans and unit offsets, so chunks can be traced back to extracted units without discarding long text. Table cells remain ordered strings; they do not become account classifications or financial figures. When a caller resubmits metadata, it is an unverified claim rather than a server attestation. No original file can be retrieved from a Quick source ID.

Temporary processing requires control over the parser's lifecycle as well as its output. A startup handshake establishes a Windows Job Object or POSIX process group before parsing. Remaining children are terminated before directory cleanup, even after successful parent exit. Receive/parser deadlines, a single process-local intake slot and document/text/reference bounds reject excess work explicitly. These controls do not establish an OS sandbox, secure erasure, real OCR quality or deployment-wide capacity.

The full PostgreSQL run passed 362 tests with one real-OCR skip. After the final locator-whitespace fix and added case, the combined Quick suite passed 86 tests, including all 50 intake cases. Source documents and OCR/draft outputs are synthetic.
